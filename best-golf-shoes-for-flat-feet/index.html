Best Golf Shoes for Flat Feet

A round of golf asks two things of your feet: about four to five miles of walking, and a stable base while you rotate hard through the swing. Flat feet struggle with both, so the right golf shoe needs walking-shoe support and a platform that doesn't let the foot roll during the turn.

What matters in a golf shoe for flat feet

  • Midfoot stability. During the swing, the trail foot rolls inward. Features like FootJoy's Stability Bridge or ECCO's stability zones resist that roll.
  • Walking comfort. A round is miles of walking. Cushioning and arch support matter as much as in a walking shoe.
  • A removable insole. For an arch-support insole or your custom orthotic.
  • Spiked or spikeless? Spiked shoes grip better on wet slopes, while spikeless shoes are more comfortable and versatile.

Walking or riding?

If you walk the course, prioritize comfort and arch support: the Skechers or ECCO with your own insole. If you mostly ride a cart, swing stability matters more than walking cushioning, which favors the FootJoy or New Balance. Either way, try golf shoes on with the socks you play in, late in the day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear orthotics in golf shoes?

Yes, if the shoe has a removable insole. The ECCO Biom Hybrid 3, for example, has a removable Ortholite insole specifically to give an extra-width option. Replace the stock insole rather than stacking the orthotic on top.

Are spikeless golf shoes OK for flat feet?

Yes. Spikeless shoes are usually more comfortable for walking, which helps flat feet over a full round. On wet or hilly courses, spiked shoes give better grip.

Do flat feet affect the golf swing?

They can. A foot that rolls inward makes it harder to keep a stable base through the rotation. A golf shoe with midfoot stability features, plus arch support, helps keep the foot steady.
