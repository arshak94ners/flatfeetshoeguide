Tools work best alongside good shoes

None of these replace a supportive shoe or insole. They work on flexibility, strength, and recovery, while your footwear handles support during the day. The combination of a supportive shoe, a daily stretching habit, and something to help recovery tends to work better than any one of them alone.

When to see a professional If pain is sharp, getting worse, keeps you awake, or hasn't improved after a few weeks of sensible self-care, see a podiatrist or physical therapist. And if you have diabetes or circulation problems, check with your doctor before using braces, splints, or massage tools.

Frequently asked questions

Do foot rollers help flat feet?

A foot roller won't change the structure of a flat foot, but rolling the sole can ease tightness and aching in the arch and plantar fascia, especially at the end of the day. It works best as part of a routine with stretching and supportive shoes.

Is a slant board good for plantar fasciitis?

Calf stretching is one of the most commonly recommended self-care steps for plantar fasciitis, and a slant board makes it easy to do consistently. Start at a gentle angle and increase gradually.

Do toe spacers fix flat feet?

No. Toe spacers spread and realign the toes, which some people find comfortable and helpful during strengthening exercises, but they don't build or support an arch on their own.
