What compression socks can (and can't) do for flat feet

They can add a gentle hug around the midfoot that many people find supportive, reduce swelling and fatigue on long days, and help a sock stay put so your foot doesn't slide inside a supportive shoe.

They can't hold up a collapsing arch the way a firm shoe or orthotic insole does. Think of them as a comfort layer on top of real support, not a replacement for it.

Check with a doctor first if… you have diabetes, peripheral artery disease, or poor circulation. Compression isn't right for everyone, and medical-grade compression should be fitted properly.

How to choose

  • For heel and arch pain: a sock or sleeve with compression focused on the arch, like Feetures or Bitly.
  • For tired calves and long runs: a knee-high compression sock like CEP.
  • For snug shoes with orthotics: a thin sock like Swiftwick, so the shoe still fits.
  • Sizing: compression only works in the right size. Use each brand's size chart, not just your shoe size.

Frequently asked questions

Do compression socks help flat feet?

They can make feet feel more supported and less fatigued, especially on long days, but they don't correct a flat arch. They work best alongside supportive shoes or insoles.

Can I wear compression socks to bed?

Light foot sleeves are sometimes worn overnight, but most compression socks are designed for daytime wear. Ask a doctor before sleeping in compression, especially if you have circulation problems.

Are plantar fasciitis socks worth it?

Many people find targeted arch compression helps with plantar fasciitis discomfort, and they're inexpensive to try. They're a supplement to stretching and supportive footwear, not a treatment on their own.
