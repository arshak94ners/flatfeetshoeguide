Why you shouldn't play in running shoes

Running shoes have tall, soft heels and flared bases designed for straight-line motion. On a court, that softness lets the foot roll over the edge of the midsole during a side step, and with flat feet that roll starts from an already-collapsed position. Court shoes are lower, firmer, and reinforced along the sides so the foot stays on top of the platform when you change direction.

The outsole matters too: court shoes use flatter, more durable rubber with patterns like herringbone that grip a hard court without catching, while running-shoe lugs can grab and twist an ankle.

What to look for with flat feet

  • Midfoot stability features (DYNAWALL, TRUSSTIC, shanks) that resist twisting.
  • Lateral reinforcement on the upper to keep the foot on the platform.
  • A removable insole so you can add an arch-support insole.
  • The right outsole for your surface: hard-court, clay, or indoor.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use tennis shoes for pickleball?

Yes. Tennis shoes are generally a good choice for pickleball because both sports need lateral support and court-specific outsoles. Pickleball-specific shoes tend to be a bit lighter and lower to the ground.

Are court shoes good for flat feet?

Court shoes with midfoot stability features and lateral reinforcement are a much better choice than running shoes for court sports with flat feet. Adding a supportive insole to a stable court shoe is a common, effective combination.

How often should I replace pickleball or tennis shoes?

Frequent players often wear through outsoles or compress midsoles within a few months. Replace them when the tread is worn smooth in the forefoot or the shoe no longer feels stable during lateral moves.
