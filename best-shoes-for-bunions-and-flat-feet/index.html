Why bunions and flat feet overlap

A bunion forms when the big toe joint is pushed outward over time, and a collapsed arch changes how weight travels across the forefoot in a way that increases exactly that kind of pressure. It's not the only cause of bunions — genetics and footwear history both play a role — but flat feet are a well-documented contributing factor, which is why treating the two together, rather than picking shoes for one and hoping the other works out, tends to give better results.

The core tension is that shoes built for aggressive arch support often use a more structured, narrower last through the forefoot, while shoes built for bunion comfort often skip real arch support entirely. The picks above were chosen specifically because they don't force that trade-off.

What actually matters for this combination

  • A wide, rounded toe box that doesn't taper sharply toward the big toe — this is the single biggest factor in bunion comfort.
  • Minimal seams or overlays directly over the bunion joint, since stitching and material overlaps are common friction points.
  • Genuine arch support that doesn't rely on a narrow, structured last to deliver it.
  • Soft, flexible upper materials like mesh or soft leather that can flex around the joint rather than pressing into it rigidly.
When shoes aren't enough For a mild, early-stage bunion, the right shoe can meaningfully reduce pain and slow progression. For a severe or rapidly worsening bunion, footwear changes alone won't reverse the joint deformity, and it's worth a podiatrist consultation about other options. Frequently asked questions

Can the right shoes fix a bunion?

The right shoes can meaningfully reduce pain and may slow how quickly a mild bunion worsens, but they generally can't reverse an existing bunion deformity. For a severe or worsening bunion, a podiatrist consultation is worth having alongside footwear changes.

Do I need a wide shoe if I have a bunion but not wide feet otherwise?

Often yes, specifically at the forefoot. A bunion pushes the big toe joint outward, which can need extra width right at that point even if the rest of your foot measures as standard width.

Are sandals better than closed shoes for bunions?

Not universally, but an adjustable sandal like the Birkenstock Arizona can be more comfortable for some people because you control exactly where the straps sit relative to the bunion. Closed shoes with a wide, rounded toe box work well too, and offer more protection.
