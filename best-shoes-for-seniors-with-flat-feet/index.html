What matters most for older adults

  • A firm heel counter and stable base. Stability helps balance, and balance helps prevent falls.
  • Arch support. Built in, or via a removable insole you can replace with an orthotic.
  • Easy closures. Hook-and-loop straps or hands-free entry, if laces are difficult.
  • A slip-resistant outsole. Rubber with good grip, not smooth or worn soles.
  • Room for swelling. A wide toe box and adjustable straps for feet that change size through the day.

About Medicare code A5500

Some shoes are listed with HCPCS code A5500, a code for diabetic shoes. People with diabetes who meet Medicare's conditions may be eligible for coverage of therapeutic shoes, usually through a prescribing doctor and a qualified supplier. Buying from Amazon doesn't by itself mean you'll be reimbursed, so ask your doctor or supplier how it works for you. See our diabetic shoes guide for more.

Talk to a doctor if… one foot is flattening more than the other, the inside of the ankle is swollen or painful, or walking is getting harder. Adult-acquired flat foot can have causes that need medical treatment, not just better shoes.

Frequently asked questions

Why do feet get flatter with age?

The tendons and ligaments that support the arch, especially the posterior tibial tendon, can stretch or weaken over time, and weight gain or conditions like arthritis add strain. Sudden or one-sided flattening should be checked by a doctor.

Are slip-on shoes safe for seniors?

Hands-free slip-ins with a heel pillow that holds the heel securely, like Skechers Slip-ins, can be a good option. Loose slip-ons or backless slippers are a fall risk and best avoided.

Does Medicare pay for shoes for flat feet?

Medicare's therapeutic shoe benefit is generally for people with diabetes who meet specific conditions, not for flat feet alone. Ask your doctor whether you qualify.
